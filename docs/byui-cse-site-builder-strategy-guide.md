# BYU-Idaho CSE Site Builder Strategy Guide

> **Scope:** Computer Science (CS) and Software Engineering (SE) only. Do not add Information Systems (IS) or other programs.

This guide helps an AI or human team build marketing pages for BYU-Idaho Computer Science and Software Engineering. It summarizes useful patterns observed at universities that are attracting students in STEM, business, and related fields.

The goal is not to copy those universities. The goal is to understand what is working and translate it into a BYU-Idaho experience that is distinctive, truthful, and consistent with the university mission.

## The central strategic idea

The strongest universities are not merely selling a degree or a list of courses. They are selling a believable transition:

> From high school student to capable adult with experience, options, relationships, and a direction.

For BYU-Idaho CSE, that transition should be expressed as:

> Learn to understand difficult problems, build useful technology, work with real people, and graduate with evidence of what you can do.

This must be joined to what makes BYU-Idaho different:

> Develop technical capability in a safe, faith-centered environment where learning, work, family, service, and personal growth can support one another.

Do not reduce the message to either career outcomes or spiritual identity. The distinctive value is the combination.

## What appears to be working elsewhere

The research notes identify several recurring patterns.

### 1. Lead with a future, not a catalog

Weak:

> We offer degrees in Computer Science and Software Engineering.

Stronger:

> You will build skills, gain experience, work with others, and graduate prepared for multiple kinds of opportunity.

The site should help visitors picture what they could become and what they could do. Program names, course lists, and requirements still matter, but they should support the larger picture.

### 2. Show proof where the promise is made

Do not make visitors search for evidence after reading a claim. Pair promises with proof such as:

- Student projects and demonstrations
- Internships and co-ops
- Graduate salary and employment data
- Job titles and industries
- Student societies and competitions
- Employer visits and professional events
- Faculty and student stories
- Course projects and portfolios
- Career preparation activities

Use verified numbers with definitions, dates, and limitations. Use placeholders when data is not yet confirmed.

### 3. Make the first experience tangible

Students respond better to experiences than to abstract descriptions of education. Show things such as:

- Building an app, game, website, or automation tool
- Solving a problem for a real user
- Working on a team project
- Presenting a project to other people
- Testing and improving a design
- Joining a society or competition
- Completing an internship
- Creating a portfolio or senior project

The site should answer, “What will I actually do?” before asking visitors to care about a curriculum map.

### 4. Make belonging part of the value

Students and parents ask:

- Will I fit here?
- Will I make friends?
- Will people like me succeed here?
- Will I be proud to attend?
- Will this place help me become the person I want to be?

Show students, relationships, teams, traditions, faculty access, societies, faith, service, and ordinary campus life. Belonging is not decoration. It lowers the emotional risk of choosing a university.

### 5. Explain affordability and value together

Do not hide cost, aid, or outcomes on distant pages. Help families understand the relationship between:

> Cost → education → experience → capability → opportunity

Use current, verified information about tuition, scholarships, internships, salaries, employment, and student support. Never imply that a salary figure is a guarantee.

### 6. Make career preparation visible

Career preparation is more believable when visitors can see where it happens. Show the experiences that develop employability, not just the claim that graduates are employable:

- Team projects
- Communication and presentations
- Technical interviews or demonstrations
- Internships
- Employer events
- Portfolios
- Project reviews
- Leadership and student organizations
- Practice with current tools

## What BYU-Idaho must preserve

The site must not become a generic version of Purdue, Georgia Tech, ASU, BYU, or another successful university. Their tactics are useful. Their identities are not ours.

### Mission and discipleship

BYU-Idaho is owned and guided by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Its mission is to develop disciples of Jesus Christ who are leaders in their homes, the Church, and their communities.

The website should reflect this through:

- Integrity and truthfulness
- Service to others
- Respect for agency
- Personal responsibility
- Growth through meaningful effort
- Faith and learning working together
- Technology used for human good

Do not force religious language into every section. Do not use sacred ideas as slogans or decorative marketing. Include explicit spiritual content when it has a genuine purpose and appropriate context.

### Safe, faith-centered environment

The university’s spiritual environment is a meaningful value proposition for many students and parents. It can be communicated through:

- A respectful campus culture
- Faith and learning existing together
- Supportive relationships
- Clear standards and expectations
- Service and community
- Opportunities for spiritual growth
- A setting where students can develop as whole people

Use current, approved evidence for safety, student well-being, Honor Code expectations, and campus resources. Do not promise that any environment is completely safe or that every student will have the same experience.

### Rexburg and Eastern Idaho lifestyle

The location itself can be part of the value proposition. Investigate and verify useful facts about:

- Campus and community safety
- Cost of living
- Outdoor recreation
- Access to Yellowstone, Jackson Hole, and regional attractions
- Community size and pace of life
- Housing and transportation
- Family-friendly environment
- Regional employers and internships
- Distance to major cities and airports

The purpose is not to pretend Rexburg is for everyone. It is to help the right students picture life there. Pair lifestyle claims with current sources and avoid vague claims such as “one of the safest places in America” unless officially documented.

### Affordability and access

Affordability should be part of the main story, not an afterthought. Use verified information about:

- Tuition
- Scholarships
- Financial aid
- Three-semester scheduling, if current
- Internships and work opportunities
- Housing and living costs
- Missionary deferment, if applicable
- Flexible pathways for transfer and returning students

Do not promise admission, scholarships, transfer credit, or a particular financial result.

### Community and personal attention

Avoid presenting BYU-Idaho as a massive, anonymous institution. Show the human scale where it is truthful:

- Faculty who know students
- Teams that build together
- Student societies
- Peer relationships
- Accessible support
- Opportunities to participate early

## How to translate outside strategies for BYU-Idaho

| Pattern elsewhere | BYU-Idaho translation | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Career certainty | Clear preparation and evidence of capability | Do not guarantee jobs or salaries |
| Elite identity | Faith, belonging, service, and purposeful work | Do not imitate prestige language |
| Future facing technology | Learn to direct powerful tools wisely | Avoid AI hype |
| Campus lifestyle | Safe, faith-centered, community-oriented Eastern Idaho experience | Verify every lifestyle and safety claim |
| Affordability | Understandable cost and opportunity story | Use current figures only |
| Employer access | Internships, projects, events, and portfolios | Show actual participation |
| Scarcity and selectivity | Welcoming access with meaningful standards | Do not manufacture urgency or exclusivity |
| Research prestige | Student creation, applied learning, and useful work | Do not overstate research capabilities |

## AI positioning

AI should be treated as an important change in the field, not the identity of the degree.

The site should acknowledge:

- AI can generate code and prototypes quickly.
- Entry-level technical work is becoming more competitive.
- Students must understand systems, users, quality, security, and tradeoffs.
- AI can support learning and productivity, but it can also weaken learning when it replaces effort and judgment.
- People remain responsible for what they build and release.

Approved direction:

> AI has changed the tools. It has not changed our responsibility for what we build.

> Use technology as a servant, not a master.

Do not say:

- AI will revolutionize everything
- AI makes the degree future proof
- Everyone must become an AI expert
- AI will guarantee better jobs
- Students can outsource the hard parts of learning

The site should welcome students who are curious about AI and students who are cautious about it. A student does not have to love every technology trend to belong in computing.

## Audience priorities

### Prospective students

Lead with:

- What they could build
- Creative freedom and useful skills
- Friends, teams, and belonging
- Real projects and experiences
- Career options without guarantees
- A safe and meaningful place to live and learn

### Current BYU-Idaho students considering a change

Lead with:

- How computing connects to their existing interests
- CS and SE comparison
- Beginner courses
- What credits may apply, subject to official evaluation
- How to meet with the right university resource
- What students can do after changing programs

### Parents

Lead with:

- Educational value and cost
- Student support and safety
- Faith and character
- Learning quality in the age of AI
- Internships and outcomes
- The skills students develop beyond code

### Transfer and returning students

Lead with:

- Clear next steps
- Credit evaluation
- Degree completion paths
- Scheduling and life flexibility
- Support for students returning after work, missions, family responsibilities, or time away

## Recommended content architecture

The homepage should create interest and trust. Supporting pages should answer practical questions in depth.

1. **Build what comes next**
2. **What our students build**
3. **Questions about AI and computing**
4. **Find your fit: CS or SE**
5. **Experience BYU-Idaho**
6. **Where this can lead**
7. **For parents and families**
8. **Current, transfer, and returning students**
9. **Your next step**

Each page should have one primary audience and one primary action.

## Design and media direction

- Use real student work, student voices, and approved campus imagery.
- Show people building, explaining, collaborating, presenting, and enjoying campus life.
- Use a curated project showcase or YouTube playlist rather than sending visitors to search a general channel.
- Use cards for comparison and discovery, not for every paragraph.
- Keep the page visually active without rapid, distracting motion.
- Use video when it demonstrates something a visitor can understand quickly.
- Use captions, transcripts, alt text, and strong color contrast.
- Follow current BYU-Idaho brand standards for logo, color, type, and photography.

## Evidence rules for the AI site builder

The AI building the site must separate three things:

1. **Approved facts**: verified and ready to publish.
2. **Claims requiring verification**: plausible but not yet documented.
3. **Placeholders**: content that needs an office, date, number, source, or approval.

Never invent:

- Salary, employment, or internship statistics
- Safety rankings
- Cost or scholarship information
- Employer relationships
- Student stories
- Course names or requirements
- Approval dates or program details
- Church statements or quotations

Every changing statistic should include its source, date, population, time period, and limitations.

## Final test for every page

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this help a student picture a worthwhile future?
2. Does it show evidence rather than make unsupported claims?
3. Does it acknowledge the real concern without hype or dismissal?
4. Does it preserve BYU-Idaho’s faith-centered identity?
5. Does it communicate the value of Rexburg and Eastern Idaho accurately?
6. Does it explain what the visitor can do next?
7. Would a student, parent, and university reviewer all recognize this as truthful?

The winning message is not that technology is safe, easy, or unchanged. It is:

> Build something real. Work with real people. Grow in a place with purpose. Graduate with evidence of what you can do and a clear direction for what comes next.
